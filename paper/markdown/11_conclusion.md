## Conclusion

### Summary of Contributions

Symergetics combines Fraction-backed arithmetic, rank-three quadray lattice
geometry, exact native volume calculations for declared polyhedral surfaces,
prime-product/digit-pattern utilities and visualization. Its contribution is a
software integration and exposition of selected topics inspired by Fuller's
Synergetics, not the invention of exact arithmetic or a universal physical theory.

Rational operations preserve rational values under their stated contracts.
Float input, irrational quantities, lattice snapping and rendering introduce
separate representation boundaries. Four quadray slots are not four independent
spatial dimensions. Native volume units must be distinguished from Cartesian
cubic units, and vertex means from uniform-solid centroids.

### Research Impact

The manuscript establishes no measured interdisciplinary impact. Active
inference, materials, biology, climate, finance and education are proposed uses
of components, not validated package deployments. No universal superiority to
Cartesian methods, symbolic tools or floating-point workflows is claimed.

### Technical Achievements

The [architecture](04_system_architecture.md) links actual implementation
modules. [Test evidence](../TEST_EVIDENCE.md) separates source inventories,
collected cases and historical execution receipts; these support their own
revisions and do not guarantee every calculation. No new comparative performance
benchmark or independent mathematical replication is reported here.

### Future Directions

The [open questions](10_ongoing_questions_inquiries.md) identify measurable
representation, resource, approximation and domain-validation studies. They are
not committed integrations. New claims should state assumptions, provide
source/data provenance and report the evidence needed to distinguish credible
alternatives.

### Broader Implications

Exact arithmetic is useful when preserving rational identities matters.
Scientific reliability additionally depends on model adequacy, measurement
uncertainty, valid geometry, numerical boundaries and appropriate validation.
A plotted pattern or exact fraction cannot replace those requirements.
