# FCC neighborhoods and scale-declared polyhedra

[Mathematical foundations](mathematical-foundations-synergetics.md) · [Geometry API](api/index.md#geometry-module)

## The Geometry of Sphere Packing

This package's IVM neighborhood is an FCC lattice generated by twelve
canonical quadrays, the permutations of `(2,1,1,0)`. Under the default
scale-one map, contact displacements have length $2\sqrt2$. Equal spheres
centered at these FCC sites touch at radius $\sqrt2$. The origin and twelve
neighbors make a **13-site local cluster**, not an infinite packing proof or
an isolated four-sphere summary. HCP also admits twelve contacts but is not
this package's FCC enumeration.

### Closest Packing and the IVM Lattice

```python
from symergetics.core.coordinates import enumerate_ivm_sites, IVM_NEIGHBORS

assert len(IVM_NEIGHBORS) == 12
assert len(enumerate_ivm_sites(1)) == 13
assert len(enumerate_ivm_sites(2)) == 55
```

`size` is graph-shell radius under these twelve moves. It is neither a Cartesian
radius nor a raw coefficient bound. Canonical IVM sites have component sum
divisible by four; arbitrary integer quadrays need not belong to this subset.
Source: [coordinates.py](../symergetics/core/coordinates.py).

### Vector Equilibrium: The Cuboctahedron

The twelve contact centers form the default cuboctahedron: twelve vertices,
twenty-four edges and fourteen faces (eight triangles and six squares).
“Vector equilibrium” is Fuller's geometric terminology; the class does not
simulate forces or prove structural stability.

## Polyhedral Relationships and Volume Ratios

| Default model | Vertices | Edges | Faces | IVM volume | Scale-one edge |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `Tetrahedron()` | 4 | 6 | 4 | 1 | $2\sqrt2$ |
| `Cube()` | 8 | 12 | 6 | 3 | 2 |
| `Octahedron()` | 6 | 12 | 8 | 4 | $2\sqrt2$ |
| `Cuboctahedron()` | 12 | 24 | 14 | 20 | $2\sqrt2$ |

These are the package's **specific default constructions**, not universal volume
ratios for arbitrary independently sized solids. In particular, cube edge two
is shorter than the reference tetrahedron edge. `volume()` is a method returning
a `Fraction`; changing `vertices` changes the measured volume. Constants are
reference values, not caches of a deformed object's geometry.

## Coordinate System Transformations

The [3 by 4 Urner map](mathematical-foundations-synergetics.md#the-rank-three-quotient)
embeds four redundant labels in three dimensions. Continuous rotations,
reflections and fractional dilations can leave the integer lattice. The
transformation helpers then quantize, so lengths, volumes and inverse round
trips need not be preserved. Exact integer translation and integer dilation
should not be conflated with those projected operations.

## Declared topology is not a convex hull

Faces use stable vertex indices and their declared triangle fans; edges come
from successive face indices. A coincident pair of physical vertices does not
erase an indexed edge. `analyze_geometry()` distinguishes indexed closure,
affine rank, unique physical vertex count, face nonplanarity and convexity.
It is **not** a self-intersection detector. The solid-volume preconditions and
centroid distinctions live in the [API contract](api/index.md#indexed-surfaces-and-live-solid-analysis).

## Visualization and Representation

Polyhedron views use scale-one XYZ displacements from the exact first vertex,
so large translations do not consume local floating precision before
subtraction. The graphical abstract's XY view projects declared edges,
including hidden or overlapping ones; it does not construct a new hull.
Metadata retains the exact anchor and live geometry measurements. See
[plotting output semantics](api/index.md#geometry-visualizations).

## Applications require an additional model

Architecture, crystallography and biological morphology can motivate geometric
experiments. The package does not supply collision detection, material
constitutive laws, stress analysis, geodesic design validation or biological
inference merely by constructing these polyhedra. See the separate
[conceptual research material](research/index.md).
